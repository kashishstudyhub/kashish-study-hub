📊 Unit 5: Phase Equilibrium & Corrosion

Gibbs Phase Rule

Gibbs phase rule gives the relationship between phases, components and degrees of freedom in an equilibrium system.

F = C - P + 2

Where:

  • F = Degrees of freedom
  • C = Number of components
  • P = Number of phases

Phase

A phase is a physically homogeneous and mechanically separable portion of a system.

Component

Components are the minimum number of chemically independent substances required to describe the composition of all phases in a system.

Water System

The water system is a one-component system because water (H₂O) is the only chemical component.

It can exist in three phases:

  • Ice – solid
  • Water – liquid
  • Water vapour – gas

C = 1

Water Phase Diagram

The phase diagram of water shows the equilibrium between ice, liquid water and water vapour at different temperatures and pressures.

Important points include:

  • Triple point
  • Fusion curve
  • Vaporization curve
  • Sublimation curve

Binary Eutectic System

A binary system contains two components. A eutectic system has a composition at which the mixture melts or solidifies at a minimum temperature.

Example:

Cu – Ag System

Copper-Silver System

The Cu-Ag system is an important binary eutectic system used to study phase equilibrium.

It contains two components:

  • Copper (Cu)
  • Silver (Ag)

The eutectic point represents the composition and temperature at which liquid transforms into two solid phases simultaneously.

Corrosion

Corrosion is the gradual deterioration of a metal due to chemical or electrochemical reaction with its environment.

Example: Rusting of iron.

Chemical Corrosion

Chemical corrosion occurs due to direct chemical reaction between a metal and a dry environment, such as gases or non-electrolytic liquids.

It is also called dry corrosion.

Electrochemical Corrosion

Electrochemical corrosion occurs when anodic and cathodic reactions take place through an electrolyte.

At the anode, metal oxidation occurs:

M → Mⁿ⁺ + ne⁻

Electrons travel through the metallic path toward the cathodic region.

Methods of Prevention

  • Protective coatings
  • Galvanization
  • Tinning
  • Cathodic protection
  • Use of corrosion inhibitors
  • Proper material selection

Galvanization

Galvanization is the process of coating iron or steel with a protective layer of zinc to reduce corrosion.

Zinc provides barrier protection and can also provide sacrificial protection to the underlying iron or steel.

Tinning

Tinning is the process of coating iron or steel with a thin layer of tin.

The tin coating acts as a barrier between the metal and the surrounding environment.

Applications

  • Food containers
  • Metal sheets
  • Household utensils

Important Formula

F = C - P + 2

For condensed systems where pressure is constant, the reduced phase rule is commonly written as:

F = C - P + 1

These formulas are used to calculate the degrees of freedom of an equilibrium system.
