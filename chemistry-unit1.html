💧 Unit 1: Water

Sources of Water

Water is obtained mainly from natural sources. These sources are:

  • Rain water
  • Surface water – rivers, lakes and ponds
  • Ground water – wells and springs
  • Sea water

Impurities in Water

Natural water contains various dissolved and suspended impurities.

  • Suspended impurities
  • Dissolved inorganic salts
  • Dissolved gases
  • Organic impurities
  • Microorganisms

Hardness of Water

Hardness is the property of water due to which it does not form lather easily with soap.

Hardness is mainly caused by calcium and magnesium salts.

Types of Hardness

  • Temporary hardness
  • Permanent hardness

Causes of Hardness

Temporary hardness is mainly due to bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium.

Permanent hardness is mainly due to chlorides and sulphates of calcium and magnesium.

Units of Hardness

Hardness is commonly expressed in terms of equivalent CaCO₃.

Hardness = mg/L as CaCO₃

Another commonly used unit is ppm.

1 ppm = 1 mg/L

Hardness Numericals

Hardness calculations generally involve converting different calcium and magnesium salts into their CaCO₃ equivalent.

CaCO₃ equivalent = Amount of substance × 50 / Equivalent weight

The total hardness is obtained by adding the individual CaCO₃ equivalents.

Determination of Hardness by EDTA Method

EDTA titration is a complexometric method used to determine the hardness of water.

EDTA forms stable complexes with Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions.

Important Reagents

  • EDTA solution
  • Buffer solution
  • Eriochrome Black-T indicator

The colour change at the end point is commonly observed from wine-red to blue.

Alkalinity

Alkalinity is the capacity of water to neutralize acids. It is mainly caused by hydroxide, carbonate and bicarbonate ions.

  • OH⁻ → Hydroxide alkalinity
  • CO₃²⁻ → Carbonate alkalinity
  • HCO₃⁻ → Bicarbonate alkalinity

Alkalinity is generally expressed as mg/L as CaCO₃.

Determination of Alkalinity

Alkalinity can be determined by acid titration using suitable indicators.

Phenolphthalein and methyl orange are commonly used indicators in alkalinity determination.

Alkalinity = Volume of acid × Normality of acid × 50,000 / Volume of water sample
