1. Introduction

Unit 4 deals with three important engineering graphics topics: Sections of Solids, Intersection of Surfaces and Development of Surfaces.

These methods are used to represent complicated three-dimensional objects in a two-dimensional engineering drawing.

2. Section of Solids

Definition

When a solid is cut by an imaginary plane, the resulting portion or surface exposed by the cutting plane is called a section of the solid.

The drawing showing the sectional shape of a solid is called a sectional view.

Why Sections Are Used?

  • To understand internal details of a solid.
  • To show hidden surfaces clearly.
  • To represent holes and cavities.
  • To simplify complicated drawings.
  • To determine the shape of the cut surface.

Section Plane Cutting a Solid

3. Section Plane

The imaginary plane used to cut a solid is called the section plane.

Types of Section Planes

  • Plane parallel to HP
  • Plane parallel to VP
  • Plane perpendicular to HP
  • Plane perpendicular to VP
  • Plane inclined to HP
  • Plane inclined to VP
  • Auxiliary section plane

Section Plane Representation

The cutting plane is generally represented using a cutting-plane line with arrows indicating the direction of viewing.

4. Sectional View

A sectional view represents the portion of the solid exposed after it is cut by a section plane.

Important Elements

  • Cutting plane
  • Sectioned surface
  • Section lines / hatching
  • Direction of viewing
  • Visible outlines

Hatching

The exposed cut surface is generally represented by thin, equally spaced inclined lines called section lines or hatching.

Sectional Representation

5. Section of a Prism

A prism may be cut by a section plane parallel, perpendicular or inclined to its axis.

General Construction Method

  1. Draw the projections of the prism.
  2. Draw the cutting plane at the specified position.
  3. Mark the points where the cutting plane intersects the edges.
  4. Project these intersection points into the required view.
  5. Join the points in their proper sequence.
  6. Hatch the sectional surface.
  7. Show the visible outlines clearly.

Section of Prism

6. Section of a Pyramid

A pyramid can be sectioned by a plane cutting one or more of its slant edges.

Procedure

  1. Draw the complete projections of the pyramid.
  2. Draw the section plane.
  3. Mark the points where the section plane intersects the edges.
  4. Project all intersection points.
  5. Join the points to obtain the sectional shape.
  6. Apply hatching.

Section of Pyramid

7. Section of a Cylinder

A cylinder may be cut by a plane parallel or inclined to its axis. The resulting section depends on the position of the cutting plane.

9. Auxiliary Sectional View

When the cutting plane is inclined to the principal reference planes, an auxiliary sectional view may be required to obtain the true shape of the section.

Purpose

  • To obtain the true shape of an inclined section.
  • To determine actual dimensions of the section.
  • To accurately represent inclined surfaces.

Method

  1. Draw the principal views.
  2. Draw the cutting plane in the required position.
  3. Mark all points of intersection.
  4. Project the points perpendicular to the auxiliary reference line.
  5. Join the projected points.
  6. Hatch the resulting sectional area.

10. Intersection of Surfaces

Definition

When two solids or surfaces meet each other, the common line or curve formed by their intersection is called the intersection of surfaces.

Examples

  • Cylinder intersecting cylinder
  • Cylinder intersecting prism
  • Pipe intersecting pipe
  • Two cylindrical surfaces intersecting each other

Applications

  • Piping systems
  • Ventilation ducts
  • Machine components
  • Sheet-metal fabrication
  • Structural components

11. Intersection of Two Cylinders

When two cylindrical surfaces penetrate each other, a common intersection curve is formed.

General Construction Method

  1. Draw the projections of both cylinders.
  2. Divide the circular surface into equal parts.
  3. Project the division points.
  4. Find the corresponding intersection points.
  5. Transfer these points to the other view.
  6. Join the points smoothly.
  7. Darken the visible portion of the intersection curve.

Intersection of Cylinders

12. Development of Surfaces

Definition

Development of a surface means unfolding the surface of a three- dimensional solid onto a plane without overlapping or changing its actual dimensions.

Purpose

  • Sheet-metal fabrication
  • Packaging
  • Pipe and duct manufacturing
  • Pattern making
  • Construction of containers
  • Manufacturing of cones and cylinders
Important:

A development represents the complete surface of a solid in a single flat pattern.

14. Parallel Line Method

Definition

In the parallel line method, the generators of the solid are parallel to each other and are transferred onto a flat surface.

Applications

  • Prism
  • Cylinder

Construction Procedure

  1. Draw the plan of the base.
  2. Divide the base into equal parts.
  3. Obtain the circumference divisions.
  4. Draw parallel generators.
  5. Transfer the true height of the solid.
  6. Join corresponding points.
  7. Add allowances if required.

Development of a Prism

15. Development of Cylinder

The development of a cylinder is a rectangle whose length is equal to the circumference of the circular base and whose height is equal to the height of the cylinder.

Length of Development = πD = 2πr
Width of Development = Height of Cylinder

Construction Steps

  1. Calculate the circumference of the base.
  2. Draw a rectangle having length equal to circumference.
  3. Take the cylinder height as the width.
  4. Divide the circumference into suitable equal parts.
  5. Transfer corresponding points.
  6. Complete the development.

Cylinder Development

16. Radial Line Method

Definition

In the radial line method, the development is constructed by drawing radial lines from a common apex or centre.

Applications

  • Pyramid
  • Cone

General Procedure

  1. Draw the projection of the solid.
  2. Determine the true length of slant edges/generators.
  3. Take the apex as the centre.
  4. Draw an arc with radius equal to true slant length.
  5. Divide the arc according to the base divisions.
  6. Join each division point to the apex.
  7. Complete the development.
