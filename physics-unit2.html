🌊 Unit 2: Wave Optics

Huygens' Principle

According to Huygens' principle, every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets. The new wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets.

  • Explains propagation of waves.
  • Useful in understanding reflection and refraction.
  • Forms the basis of wave optics.

Interference

Interference is the redistribution of intensity that occurs when two or more coherent light waves overlap.

Types

  • Constructive interference
  • Destructive interference

Constructive: Path difference = nλ

Destructive: Path difference = (2n + 1)λ/2

Coherent Sources

Two sources are coherent when they emit waves of the same frequency and maintain a constant phase difference.

Coherent sources are essential for obtaining sustained interference fringes.

Young's Experiment

Young's double-slit experiment demonstrates the wave nature of light through interference.

Fringe Width: β = λD/d

  • λ = Wavelength
  • D = Distance between slits and screen
  • d = Distance between two slits

Newton's Rings

Newton's rings are concentric interference fringes produced by the thin air film between a curved lens and a plane glass plate.

Dₙ² = 4nλR

where Dₙ is the diameter of the nth dark ring and R is the radius of curvature of the lens.

Michelson Interferometer

Michelson interferometer is an optical instrument used to produce and study interference fringes.

Applications

  • Measurement of wavelength
  • Measurement of small distances
  • Determination of refractive index
  • Precision optical measurements

Mach-Zehnder Interferometer

Mach-Zehnder interferometer divides a light beam into two paths and then recombines them to produce interference.

It is useful for studying changes in optical path length and refractive index.

Diffraction

Diffraction is the bending and spreading of waves when they pass through an aperture or around an obstacle whose dimensions are comparable to the wavelength.

Types

  • Fresnel diffraction
  • Fraunhofer diffraction

Fraunhofer Diffraction

Fraunhofer diffraction is observed when the source and observation point are effectively at infinity, commonly achieved using lenses.

Single Slit Minima: a sinθ = nλ

where a is the slit width and n = 1, 2, 3, ...

Rayleigh Criterion

Rayleigh criterion gives a condition for two point sources to be just resolved by an optical instrument.

θmin = 1.22 λ/D

where D is the diameter of the circular aperture.

Diffraction Grating

A diffraction grating consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits or lines.

d sinθ = nλ

This relation gives the principal maxima for normal incidence.

Applications

  • Measurement of wavelength
  • Spectral analysis
  • Resolution of spectral lines
