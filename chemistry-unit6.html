🔬 Unit 6: Spectroscopic Techniques

What is Spectroscopy?

Spectroscopy is the study of the interaction between electromagnetic radiation and matter.

It is used to identify substances and study their molecular structure.

Electromagnetic Radiation

Electromagnetic radiation travels through space in the form of electric and magnetic fields.

c = νλ

Where:

  • c = Speed of light
  • ν = Frequency
  • λ = Wavelength

UV-Visible Spectroscopy

UV-Visible spectroscopy measures the absorption of ultraviolet and visible radiation by a substance.

It is commonly associated with electronic transitions in molecules.

A = εcl

This is the Beer-Lambert law.

  • A = Absorbance
  • ε = Molar absorptivity
  • c = Concentration
  • l = Path length

Principle

When a molecule absorbs UV or visible radiation, an electron can be promoted from a lower-energy molecular orbital to a higher-energy molecular orbital.

The wavelength of maximum absorption is called λmax.

Main Components

  1. Radiation source
  2. Monochromator
  3. Sample holder
  4. Detector
  5. Display/recording system

The instrument measures how much radiation is absorbed by the sample.

Applications

  • Determination of concentration
  • Identification of absorbing species
  • Study of electronic transitions
  • Analysis of pharmaceutical compounds
  • Environmental and chemical analysis

Infrared Spectroscopy

IR spectroscopy studies the absorption of infrared radiation by molecules.

It is mainly used to study molecular vibrations and identify functional groups.

Principle

Molecules absorb infrared radiation at frequencies corresponding to their vibrational motions.

Different chemical bonds absorb at characteristic frequencies, making IR spectroscopy useful for identifying molecular structures.

Types of Molecular Vibrations

  • Stretching vibration
  • Bending vibration

Stretching

Stretching involves a change in bond length.

Bending

Bending involves a change in bond angle.

Diatomic Molecules

A diatomic molecule contains two atoms. Examples include HCl, CO and NO.

A diatomic molecule can show vibrational absorption when the vibration produces a change in dipole moment.

Important: Homonuclear diatomic molecules such as H₂ and N₂ do not show ordinary IR vibrational absorption because their vibration does not produce a change in dipole moment.

Main Components

  1. IR radiation source
  2. Wavelength selector / interferometer
  3. Sample holder
  4. Detector
  5. Signal processing system
  6. Display

Applications

  • Identification of functional groups
  • Identification of compounds
  • Study of molecular structure
  • Purity testing
  • Qualitative chemical analysis

Comparison

  • UV-Visible: Mainly studies electronic transitions.
  • IR: Mainly studies molecular vibrational transitions.
  • UV-Visible is widely used for quantitative concentration measurements.
  • IR is widely used for identifying functional groups and compounds.
