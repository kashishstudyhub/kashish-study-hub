🧪 Unit 4: Polymer & Polymerization

Polymer

A polymer is a high molecular mass substance formed by joining many small molecules called monomers.

Polymerization

Polymerization is the chemical process by which monomers combine to form polymers.

Monomer + Monomer + Monomer → Polymer

Types of Polymerization

  • Addition Polymerization
  • Condensation Polymerization

Addition Polymerization

Unsaturated monomers combine without elimination of small molecules.

Condensation Polymerization

Monomers combine with elimination of small molecules such as water or HCl.

Free Radical Polymerization

Free radical polymerization is a chain-growth polymerization mechanism involving free radicals.

Main Steps

  1. Initiation
  2. Propagation
  3. Termination

It is commonly used for polymerization of vinyl monomers.

Ionic Polymerization

Ionic polymerization is a chain-growth process in which the active centre is an ion.

  • Cationic polymerization
  • Anionic polymerization

The mechanism depends on the nature of the monomer and catalyst or initiator.

PVC

PVC is produced by polymerization of vinyl chloride monomer.

n(CH₂=CHCl) → [–CH₂–CHCl–]ₙ

Uses

  • Pipes
  • Cable insulation
  • Flooring
  • Sheets and films

Teflon

Teflon, or PTFE, is produced by polymerization of tetrafluoroethylene.

n(CF₂=CF₂) → [–CF₂–CF₂–]ₙ

Properties

  • High chemical resistance
  • Low coefficient of friction
  • Good thermal stability
  • Non-stick surface

Nylon

Nylon is a synthetic polyamide polymer. Important types include Nylon-6 and Nylon-6,6.

Uses

  • Textile fibres
  • Ropes
  • Engineering plastics
  • Machine components

Thermoplastics

Thermoplastics soften on heating and harden on cooling. This process can generally be repeated.

Examples:

  • PVC
  • Polyethylene
  • Polystyrene

Thermosetting Polymers

Thermosetting polymers form a permanent cross-linked structure when cured and do not soften again on reheating.

Examples:

  • Bakelite
  • Melamine-formaldehyde
  • Epoxy resins

Difference

  • Thermoplastic: Can soften on heating and generally be reshaped.
  • Thermosetting: Once cured, cannot be remoulded by heating.
  • Thermosetting polymers generally have extensive cross-linking.

Vulcanization of Rubber

Vulcanization is the process of heating rubber with sulfur to form cross-links between polymer chains.

Advantages

  • Improves strength
  • Improves elasticity
  • Improves abrasion resistance
  • Improves temperature resistance
