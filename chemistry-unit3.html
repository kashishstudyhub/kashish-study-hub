🛢️ Unit 3: Lubricants and Lubrication

Lubrication

Lubrication is the process of reducing friction and wear between two surfaces in relative motion by introducing a suitable lubricant between them.

Lubricant

A lubricant is a substance used to reduce friction, wear and heat between moving surfaces.

Functions

  • Reduces friction
  • Reduces wear and tear
  • Removes heat
  • Prevents corrosion
  • Provides sealing
  • Removes contaminants in some systems

Classification

Lubricants are commonly classified as:

  • Liquid lubricants
  • Semisolid lubricants
  • Solid lubricants

Examples include lubricating oils, greases, graphite and molybdenum disulfide.

Mechanisms of Lubrication

  • Hydrodynamic lubrication
  • Boundary lubrication
  • Extreme-pressure lubrication

Hydrodynamic Lubrication

A continuous lubricant film separates the moving surfaces and prevents direct metal-to-metal contact.

Boundary Lubrication

A thin adsorbed or chemically formed film protects surfaces when a full fluid film cannot be maintained.

Extreme-Pressure Lubrication

Special additives form protective films under very high loads and temperatures.

Viscosity

Viscosity is the resistance offered by a fluid to the relative motion between its layers.

It is an important property used to select lubricating oils.

Viscosity Index

Viscosity index indicates how much the viscosity of an oil changes with temperature.

A higher viscosity index generally indicates a smaller change in viscosity with temperature.

Flash Point

Flash point is the lowest temperature at which an oil gives off enough vapour to ignite momentarily when exposed to a test flame.

Fire Point

Fire point is the lowest temperature at which the vapour of the oil continues to burn after ignition under specified test conditions.

Generally, the fire point is higher than the flash point.

Cloud Point

Cloud point is the temperature at which wax crystals first become visible and the oil begins to appear cloudy on cooling.

Pour Point

Pour point is the lowest temperature at which an oil can flow under specified test conditions.

Acid Number

Acid number is the number of milligrams of KOH required to neutralize the acidic constituents present in one gram of oil.

Acid Number = mg of KOH required / weight of oil in grams

A high acid number can indicate the presence of acidic substances in the oil.

Saponification Number

Saponification number is the number of milligrams of KOH required to saponify the esters and neutralize the free acids present in one gram of oil.

Saponification Number = mg of KOH / gram of oil
