⚛️ Unit 7: Periodic Properties

Periodic Properties

Periodic properties are the properties of elements that show regular variations with atomic number in the periodic table.

Important periodic properties include atomic size, ionization energy, electron affinity and electronegativity.

Atomic Size

Atomic size is related to the distance between the nucleus and the outermost region of the electron cloud.

Trend

  • Across a period: atomic size generally decreases.
  • Down a group: atomic size generally increases.

The increase down a group is mainly due to the addition of new electron shells.

Ionic Size

Ionic size refers to the size of an ion.

  • Cations are generally smaller than their parent atoms.
  • Anions are generally larger than their parent atoms.

In an isoelectronic series, ionic size generally decreases as nuclear charge increases.

Ionization Energy

Ionization energy is the minimum energy required to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom in its ground state.

Trend

  • Across a period: generally increases.
  • Down a group: generally decreases.
Successive ionization energies are generally greater than the preceding ones.

Electron Affinity

Electron affinity describes the energy change associated with adding an electron to an isolated gaseous atom.

General Trend

  • It generally becomes more favorable across a period.
  • Trends down a group are less uniform.

Halogens generally have highly favorable electron gain because they are one electron short of a noble-gas configuration.

Electronegativity

Electronegativity is the tendency of an atom in a molecule to attract the shared electrons toward itself.

Trend

  • Across a period: generally increases.
  • Down a group: generally decreases.

Fluorine has the highest electronegativity on the Pauling scale.

Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff)

Effective nuclear charge is the net positive charge experienced by an electron after considering the shielding effect of other electrons.

Zeff ≈ Z − S

  • Z = Nuclear charge
  • S = Shielding constant

Effective nuclear charge generally increases across a period.

Shielding Effect

Shielding effect occurs because inner-shell electrons partially reduce the attraction between the nucleus and outer electrons.

Shielding generally increases as the number of occupied electron shells increases down a group.

Oxidation State

Oxidation state is the apparent charge assigned to an atom in a compound according to standard electron-assignment rules.

Examples

  • Na in NaCl = +1
  • Cl in NaCl = −1
  • O in H₂O = −2
  • H in H₂O = +1

Transition elements often show multiple oxidation states.

Periodic Trends

  • Atomic size: decreases across a period and increases down a group.
  • Ionization energy: generally increases across a period and decreases down a group.
  • Electronegativity: generally increases across a period and decreases down a group.
  • Shielding effect: generally increases down a group.
  • Effective nuclear charge: generally increases across a period.
