1. Introduction to Engineering Graphics

What is Engineering Graphics?

Engineering Graphics is the graphical language used by engineers to represent engineering objects, machines, buildings, components and technical ideas.

An engineering drawing provides information about the shape, size, dimensions, position and construction of an object.

Importance

  • Provides a common language between engineers and technicians.
  • Helps understand shape and size of objects.
  • Useful in manufacturing and production.
  • Helps communicate technical ideas.
  • Develops visualization skills.
  • Provides the foundation for CAD.

Principles of Engineering Drawing

  1. Accuracy: Drawing should represent the object correctly.
  2. Clarity: Information should be easy to understand.
  3. Neatness: Drawing should be clean.
  4. Uniformity: Standard conventions should be followed.
  5. Proportion: Object should be represented properly.
  6. Simplicity: Unnecessary details should be avoided.

Applications

  • Mechanical Engineering
  • Civil Engineering
  • Electrical Engineering
  • Architecture
  • Manufacturing
  • Computer Aided Design

3. Lettering

Definition

Lettering is the process of writing letters, numbers and symbols on an engineering drawing in a clear and standard manner.

Single Stroke Lettering

Single stroke lettering means producing letters with approximately uniform thickness of stroke.

Characteristics

  • Letters should be clear.
  • Height should be uniform.
  • Spacing should be uniform.
  • Stroke thickness should be approximately uniform.
  • Letters should not overlap.

Single Stroke Lettering

4. Dimensioning

Definition

Dimensioning is the process of indicating the size and location of features of an object on an engineering drawing.

Elements of Dimensioning

  • Dimension line
  • Extension line
  • Arrowheads
  • Dimension figures
  • Leader line
  • Centre line

Methods of Dimensioning

1. Unidirectional

All dimension figures are written horizontally and are read from the bottom of the drawing.

2. Aligned

Dimension figures are written parallel to the dimension line.

3. Chain Dimensioning

Dimensions are arranged consecutively from one feature to another.

4. Parallel Dimensioning

Several dimensions are measured from a common reference line.

5. Scales

Definition

A scale is a proportion used to represent an object smaller, equal to, or larger than its actual size.

Types of Scales

  • Plain Scale
  • Diagonal Scale
  • Scale of Chords
  • Vernier Scale

Representative Factor (RF)

Representative Factor is the ratio of the length of an object on the drawing to its actual length, with both quantities expressed in the same units.

RF = Length of Drawing / Actual Length

Types according to RF

  • RF < 1: Reducing scale
  • RF = 1: Full-size scale
  • RF > 1: Enlarging scale

Example

If 1 cm on drawing represents 1 metre in actual size:

RF = 1 / 100

6. Plain Scale

A plain scale is used to measure two units or one unit and its subdivision.

Construction Procedure

  1. Find maximum length to be measured.
  2. Calculate RF.
  3. Calculate length of scale.
  4. Draw the base line.
  5. Divide the line into main divisions.
  6. Subdivide the required division.
  7. Write numerical values.
  8. Mark the required measurement.

Plain Scale

7. Diagonal Scale

A diagonal scale is used when smaller subdivisions than those available on a plain scale are required.

It works on the principle of similar triangles.

Construction Procedure

  1. Calculate RF.
  2. Find the required length of scale.
  3. Draw the base line.
  4. Divide it into main divisions.
  5. Construct a rectangle above the scale.
  6. Divide the vertical side into equal parts.
  7. Draw diagonal lines.
  8. Use diagonal intersections for smaller measurements.

Diagonal Scale

8. Scale of Chords

Scale of chords is a graphical scale used for construction and measurement of angles.

Principle

It is based on the chord lengths corresponding to different angles in a circle.

Uses

  • Construction of angles.
  • Measurement of angles.
  • Useful when a protractor is unavailable.

Scale of Chords

9. Vernier Scale

A vernier scale is an auxiliary scale used for measuring small dimensions with greater accuracy.

Important Terms

  • Main Scale: The primary graduated scale.
  • Vernier Scale: The auxiliary scale used with the main scale.
  • Least Count: The smallest measurement that can be measured.
  • Vernier Division: A division on the vernier scale.

Applications

  • Precision measurement
  • Machine components
  • Engineering instruments
  • Technical drawings

11. Ellipse

Definition

An ellipse is a closed curve in which the sum of the distances of any point from two fixed points called foci is constant.

Important Parts

  • Major Axis
  • Minor Axis
  • Centre
  • Focus
  • Vertices

Ellipse Diagram

Ellipse Construction — General Method

  1. Draw the major axis AB.
  2. Draw the minor axis CD perpendicular to AB.
  3. Divide the major axis into suitable equal parts.
  4. Divide the minor axis into corresponding parts.
  5. Draw construction lines.
  6. Locate points on the ellipse.
  7. Repeat for all quadrants.
  8. Join the points with a smooth curve.

Properties

  • It is a closed curve.
  • It has two axes.
  • It has two foci.
  • It has two major vertices.
  • It is symmetrical about both axes.

12. Parabola

Definition

A parabola is a curve in which every point is equidistant from a fixed point called the focus and a fixed straight line called the directrix.

Important Parts

  • Focus
  • Directrix
  • Axis
  • Vertex

Parabola

Construction Procedure

  1. Draw the directrix.
  2. Draw the axis perpendicular to the directrix.
  3. Locate the focus.
  4. Locate the vertex.
  5. Take suitable points on the directrix.
  6. Draw perpendicular construction lines.
  7. Use the focus to locate corresponding points.
  8. Repeat for different points.
  9. Join the points smoothly.

13. Hyperbola

Definition

A hyperbola is an open curve consisting of two branches. The difference between the distances of any point from two fixed points called foci is constant.

Important Parts

  • Transverse Axis
  • Conjugate Axis
  • Centre
  • Focus
  • Vertices
  • Asymptotes

Hyperbola

Construction Procedure

  1. Draw the transverse axis.
  2. Draw the conjugate axis.
  3. Mark the required points.
  4. Draw construction lines.
  5. Locate points on both branches.
  6. Join the points smoothly.

14. Rectangular Hyperbola

Definition

A rectangular hyperbola is a special type of hyperbola in which the two asymptotes are perpendicular to each other.

Important Features

  • It has two branches.
  • Its asymptotes are perpendicular.
  • The branches are symmetrical.
  • The curve approaches the asymptotes.

Construction Procedure

  1. Draw two perpendicular asymptotes.
  2. Mark suitable points on the asymptotes.
  3. Draw horizontal and vertical construction lines.
  4. Locate corresponding points.
  5. Repeat for different points.
  6. Join the points smoothly.
  7. Complete the other branch by symmetry.

Rectangular Hyperbola

15. Tangents and Normals

Tangent

A tangent is a straight line that touches a curve at one point. That point is called the point of contact.

Normal

A normal is a straight line drawn perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact.

Important Relationship

Normal ⟂ Tangent

Uses

  • Used in conic construction problems.
  • Used for determining direction of curves.
  • Important in engineering drawing problems.

Tangent and Normal
