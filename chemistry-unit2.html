⚙️ Unit 2: Boiler Problems & Softening Methods

Scale

Scale is a hard and adherent deposit formed on the inner walls of a boiler.

It is mainly caused by salts such as calcium carbonate, calcium sulphate and magnesium hydroxide.

Disadvantages

  • Reduces heat transfer
  • Increases fuel consumption
  • May cause overheating of boiler metal
  • Reduces boiler efficiency

Sludge

Sludge is a soft, loose and non-adherent deposit formed inside a boiler.

It generally accumulates in comparatively cooler parts of the boiler.

Prevention

  • Using properly treated water
  • Regular blow-down operation
  • External water treatment

Foaming

Foaming is the formation of a layer of stable bubbles or foam on the surface of boiler water.

It is commonly associated with high alkalinity, dissolved organic matter and certain impurities.

Prevention

  • Control of alkalinity
  • Removal of oily impurities
  • Proper blow-down

Boiler Corrosion

Boiler corrosion is the gradual destruction of boiler metal due to chemical or electrochemical reactions with water and dissolved substances.

Causes

  • Dissolved oxygen
  • Carbon dioxide
  • Acidic conditions

Control

  • Removal of dissolved gases
  • Use of suitable treatment chemicals
  • Proper water treatment

Caustic Embrittlement

Caustic embrittlement is a form of boiler damage associated with concentrated alkaline conditions, particularly sodium hydroxide, at stressed parts of the boiler.

Prevention

  • Control excessive alkalinity
  • Use phosphate conditioning where appropriate
  • Maintain proper boiler-water chemistry

Lime-Soda Process

In the lime-soda process, calculated quantities of lime Ca(OH)₂ and soda ash Na₂CO₃ are added to hard water.

Calcium and magnesium ions are converted into insoluble precipitates that can be removed.

Advantages

  • Suitable for large-scale water treatment
  • Reduces hardness
  • Relatively economical

Zeolite Process

The zeolite process removes Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions by exchanging them with Na⁺ ions present in the zeolite.

Na₂Z + Ca²⁺ → CaZ + 2Na⁺

Regeneration

Exhausted zeolite can be regenerated using concentrated sodium chloride solution.

Ion-Exchange Process

Ion exchange is used to remove dissolved ionic impurities from water.

A cation exchanger replaces positive ions such as Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ with H⁺ ions.

An anion exchanger replaces anions with OH⁻ ions.

H⁺ and OH⁻ combine to form H₂O, producing demineralized water.

Water Softening Numericals

Important numerical problems are based on:

  • Lime requirement
  • Soda requirement
  • Hardness calculation
  • Zeolite requirement
  • Ion-exchange calculations
