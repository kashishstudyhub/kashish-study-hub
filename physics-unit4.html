LASER

LASER stands for Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation.

A laser produces a highly directional and coherent beam of electromagnetic radiation.

Important Properties

  • High coherence
  • High directionality
  • High intensity
  • Nearly monochromatic output

Spontaneous Emission

Spontaneous emission occurs when an excited atom returns to a lower energy state on its own and emits a photon.

E₂ − E₁ = hν

Stimulated Emission

Stimulated emission occurs when an incoming photon of the appropriate energy causes an excited atom to emit another photon.

The emitted photon has the same frequency, phase, direction and polarization as the stimulating photon in the idealized laser transition.

Einstein Coefficients

Einstein introduced coefficients to describe absorption, spontaneous emission and stimulated emission.

  • B₁₂: Absorption
  • A₂₁: Spontaneous emission
  • B₂₁: Stimulated emission

These coefficients are fundamental to the theory of laser action.

Population Inversion

Population inversion is a condition in which the number of atoms in an upper laser level is greater than the number in the lower laser level.

N₂ > N₁

Population inversion is necessary for net stimulated emission and laser amplification.

Optical Pumping

Pumping is the process of supplying energy to the active medium to produce population inversion.

Examples

  • Optical pumping
  • Electrical pumping
  • Current injection

Ruby Laser

Ruby laser is a solid-state laser in which a ruby crystal acts as the active medium.

  • Active medium: Ruby crystal
  • Pumping: Optical pumping
  • It operates as a pulsed laser.

Helium-Neon Laser

He-Ne laser is a gas laser containing helium and neon gases.

  • Active medium: He-Ne gas mixture
  • Electrical discharge is used for excitation.
  • It commonly produces a continuous visible beam.

Semiconductor Laser

A semiconductor laser is based on stimulated emission in a semiconductor junction.

  • Small size
  • High efficiency
  • Direct electrical pumping
  • Widely used in optical communication

Optical Fiber

An optical fiber is a thin, flexible dielectric waveguide that transmits light through the principle of total internal reflection.

Main Parts

  • Core
  • Cladding
  • Protective coating

Total Internal Reflection

Light is guided through an optical fiber by repeated total internal reflection at the core-cladding boundary.

For total internal reflection:

  • Light must travel from denser to rarer medium.
  • Angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle.

Numerical Aperture

Numerical aperture represents the light-gathering ability of an optical fiber.

NA = √(n₁² − n₂²)

For a fiber in air:

NA = sin θₐ

  • n₁ = Refractive index of core
  • n₂ = Refractive index of cladding
  • θₐ = Acceptance angle

Acceptance Angle

The acceptance angle is the maximum angle, measured with respect to the fiber axis, at which light can enter the fiber and still be guided.

NA = sin θₐ

Types of Fiber

  • Single-mode fiber
  • Multimode fiber
  • Step-index fiber
  • Graded-index fiber

Single-mode fibers support one principal propagation mode, while multimode fibers support many modes.

Applications

  • Optical fiber communication
  • Medical applications
  • Industrial measurement
  • Barcode scanners
  • Laser printing
  • Data transmission
  • Scientific instruments

Photon Energy: E = hν

Population Inversion: N₂ > N₁

Numerical Aperture: NA = √(n₁² − n₂²)

For air: NA = sin θₐ
