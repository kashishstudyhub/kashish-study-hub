🔷 Unit 3: Introduction to Solids

Solid State

A solid has a definite shape and volume because its particles are strongly bound.

Solids can be broadly classified as crystalline and amorphous.

  • Crystalline solids have long-range order.
  • Amorphous solids have mainly short-range order.

Free Electron Theory

Free electron theory treats conduction electrons in a metal as approximately free to move through the solid.

Basic Ideas

  • Metals contain mobile conduction electrons.
  • Positive ions form the lattice.
  • Electrons contribute to electrical conduction.
  • Electron motion is influenced by collisions.

Sommerfeld Model

The quantum free electron model applies quantum mechanics to conduction electrons in metals.

Electrons occupy allowed quantum states according to the Pauli exclusion principle and Fermi-Dirac statistics.

Fermi Energy

Fermi energy is the energy of the highest occupied state at absolute zero temperature.

EF = (ℏ²/2m)(3π²n)2/3

Here n is the electron number density.

At absolute zero, states below the Fermi energy are occupied and states above it are unoccupied for an ideal free-electron gas.

Density of States (DOS)

Density of states represents the number of allowed quantum states available per unit energy interval.

For a three-dimensional free-electron system, the density of states increases with the square root of energy.

g(E) ∝ √E

Energy Bands

In a solid, the closely spaced atomic energy levels combine to form allowed energy bands separated by forbidden energy gaps.

Important Bands

  • Valence band
  • Conduction band
  • Forbidden energy gap

Classification Based on Band Structure

Conductors: Have partially filled bands or overlapping valence and conduction bands.

Semiconductors: Have a relatively small band gap.

Insulators: Have a relatively large band gap.

Bloch's Theorem

Bloch's theorem describes the wavefunctions of electrons moving in a periodic crystal potential.

ψk(r) = uk(r)eikr

Here uk(r) has the periodicity of the crystal lattice.

Kronig-Penney Model

The Kronig-Penney model is a simplified one-dimensional model used to explain the formation of allowed energy bands and forbidden energy gaps in crystals.

  • Uses a periodic potential.
  • Shows allowed energy regions.
  • Shows forbidden energy gaps.
  • Helps explain electronic band structure.

Formation of Energy Bands

When many identical atoms are brought close together to form a solid, their discrete atomic energy levels split into a very large number of closely spaced levels.

These closely spaced levels form energy bands.

Allowed Band → Energy states available

Forbidden Gap → Energy range with no allowed states

Energy Gap

The forbidden energy gap is the energy range between the top of the valence band and the bottom of the conduction band where no allowed electronic states exist in the ideal band picture.

Eg = EC − EV

  • EC = conduction-band edge
  • EV = valence-band edge

Important Points

Fermi Energy: EF = (ℏ²/2m)(3π²n)2/3

Density of States: g(E) ∝ √E

Bloch Function: ψk(r) = uk(r)eikr

Band Gap: Eg = EC − EV
